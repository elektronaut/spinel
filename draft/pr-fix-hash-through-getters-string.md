title: String#[]= and insert through a reader reach the ivar

Fixes #N.

A String ivar that is mutated in place can be promoted to a shared handle (#3227), so that a reader hands out the ivar's own string and `c.name << "!"` lands on the ivar. Which mutators trigger that is decided by `sp_str_mutator`: each name carries a mask of the receiver shapes it can serve, and `SP_MUT_IVAR` is the one that makes a reader demand the handle. `[]=` and `insert` did not carry it, because their codegen rewrites the receiver through a rename-and-shadow shim that only exists for locals and ivar reads. So through a reader, the reader returned a plain string, `[]=` found no arm that takes a call-node receiver and ended in the NoMethodError fallback, and `insert` mutated a copy that was then thrown away.

`[]=` and `insert` now carry `SP_MUT_IVAR`, so a reader they mutate hands out the handle. A new shim over a reader call (`sb_reader_shim_open`/`sb_reader_shim_close` in `src/codegen_util.c`) does for a call node what the local and ivar shims do for a name: it takes the handle's slot, makes the call node read as a shadow copy `lv__sbN` through the argument-override table, clears the handle marks for the duration, and re-runs the existing value-semantics arm, after which the shadow's bytes are written back into the handle with `sp_String_set_bin` (raising FrozenError first if the handle is frozen). `emit_array_mutate_stmt` uses it in statement position; `sb_reader_expr_shim` in `src/codegen_call_recv.c` uses it in value position (`t = w.s.insert(1, "-")`), and the value-position `[]=` arm in `src/codegen_call.c` accepts a reader call that hands out the handle. The arms that check whether the receiver is assignable treat the shadowed reader call as assignable (`sb_shadowed_reader`). `slice!` and `setbyte` deliberately stay out of `SP_MUT_IVAR`: they have no value-position shim over a reader yet. Readers mutated only by the existing ivar mutators, and `[]=`/`insert` on locals and ivars, go through the same arms as before.

Tests: `test/string_index_assign_through_reader.rb` is the issue's first reproducer; an `attr_reader` String written with `s[i, n] = v`, `s["sub"] = v`, `s[range] = v` and `insert`, then `[]=` and `insert` in value position with their results printed; and two unrelated classes with the same `attr_reader` name mutated through a block parameter. It fails on master and matches CRuby 4.0 with this change. `make gate` is clean apart from the two known sandbox failures, `pkg.tmpdir.tmpdir_expand_usable` and `socket_ipv6_and_class_methods`.

Not covered: `slice!` and `setbyte` through a reader are still silently dropped, as on master; they need value-position shims of their own (for `slice!`'s String and Range forms, and in `emit_scalar_call` for `setbyte`). A class that calls `setbyte` on the ivar anywhere also keeps the ivar from being promoted to a handle, which drops `[]=` and `insert` through its reader too. Separately, a local alias taken before a mutation through the reader (`x = c.name; c.name[0] = "X"`) still does not see the change, as with `<<` on master.
