title: *nil spreads to nothing into a rest, and a scalar splat binds as one argument in dispatch

Fixes #N.

Splat lowering had two gaps. First, when `emit_rest_pack_kwh` packs the arguments for a `*rest` parameter, a splat operand that is statically nil fell through to the scalar branch and was pushed as one element, so `r(*nil)` gave `[nil]` where CRuby gives `[]`, and `def q(a, *r)` called `q(1, *nil, *[2, 3])` packed `[nil, 2, 3]` for `r`. The suffix loop of `emit_rest_from_splat_and_argv` (the arguments after a leading splat) did the same, and also pushed a boxed (poly or unknown) operand whole instead of spreading it. That loop also rendered every splat operand up front, even in the branches that never used the render, and a render writes the operand's setup into the prelude. Second, the instance-dispatch splat layout in `emit_dispatch` (`splat_at_d`) sent only a poly or unknown operand through the splat's own normalization (`sp_splat_to_array`). A nil or scalar operand was taken for an array, so `O.new.m(*7)` bound `[7]` to `a`, and `o.g(*5)` on `def g(x) = x + 1` raised "no implicit conversion of Integer into Array". This is the same one-line gap #4898 closed in `emit_args_filled`.

`emit_rest_pack_kwh` now has a nil branch that evaluates the operand for its side effects and pushes nothing. The suffix loop in `emit_rest_from_splat_and_argv` renders the operand only for the typed-array branches, and sends a poly, unknown or nil operand through the splat node's own lowering, which spreads an array and turns nil into nothing. In `emit_dispatch` a scalar operand (`splat_operand_is_scalar`, which includes nil) is normalized the same way as a boxed one. Typed array operands take the same paths as before, and a scalar splat into a rest (`r(*5)`) is still one element.

Tests: `test/splat_nil_scalar_into_rest.rb` covers `*nil` before, after and between other arguments into a rest, a nil-returning method as the operand (it still runs), a leading splat followed by `*nil` and by a poly array, and on an instance: `o.m(*7)`, `o.m(*"s")`, `*nil` and `*5` into a rest, `o.g(*5)`, and `o.m(*nil)` raising `ArgumentError` for too few arguments. It fails on master and matches CRuby 4.0 with this change. `make gate` is clean apart from the two known sandbox failures, `pkg.tmpdir.tmpdir_expand_usable` and `socket_ipv6_and_class_methods`.
